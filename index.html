A Comparative Analysis of File System Artifact Recovery in Windows 11 NTFS and ReFS Environments: Implications for Enterprise Digital Forensic Investigations

Abstract

This study presents a comprehensive comparative analysis of artifact recovery rates, metadata preservation fidelity, and journaling integrity across 847 distinct forensic images derived from controlled laboratory environments simulating enterprise Windows 11 deployments. Using a novel Forensic Resilience Index (FRI) — a composite scoring metric integrating $MFT completeness, $LogFile continuity, journal sequence coherence, and timestamp accuracy — we systematically evaluated New Technology File System (NTFS) and Resilient File System (ReFS) performance under six categories of anti-forensic pressure: secure deletion, volume shadow copy manipulation, journaling disruption, metadata timestomping, cluster reallocation, and encryption-at-rest. Our findings reveal that NTFS volumes (mean FRI: 0.647, σ = 0.091) consistently outperform ReFS (mean FRI: 0.581, σ = 0.114) in conventional artifact recovery scenarios. However, ReFS demonstrates statistically superior journal integrity under high write-volume conditions (p < 0.001), owing to its native B+ tree allocation and block-clone semantics. We further document 14 previously unreported artifact generation pathways within the ReFS 3.7 specification and propose a revised forensic workflow adapted for hybrid NTFS/ReFS enterprise environments. These findings have material implications for chain-of-custody procedures, tool validation protocols, and expert witness testimony in jurisdictions adopting Windows Server 2022 and Windows 11 24H2.

Keywords: digital forensics NTFS ReFS 3.7 file system analysis anti-forensics Windows 11 24H2 Forensic Resilience Index chain of custody artifact recovery enterprise investigation

1. Introduction

The evidentiary landscape of modern digital forensics is inextricably bound to the architectural idiosyncrasies of contemporary file systems. As enterprise computing environments increasingly migrate from the decades-old New Technology File System (NTFS) to the comparatively nascent Resilient File System (ReFS), practitioners and researchers alike face a pivotal reconfiguration of investigative assumptions, toolchain dependencies, and courtroom testimony standards.[1]

Microsoft introduced ReFS in Windows Server 2012, positioned primarily as a high-resilience alternative for storage spaces and Hyper-V workloads. Subsequent revisions — culminating in the ReFS 3.7 specification bundled with Windows Server 2022 and Windows 11 24H2 — have progressively expanded its enterprise adoption footprint. Yet the forensic literature has struggled to keep pace with this structural evolution. A systematic review of publications indexed in Web of Science and Scopus between 2012 and 2024 reveals that fewer than 3.2% of peer-reviewed digital forensics studies specifically address ReFS artifact generation, compared to 41.7% addressing NTFS in equivalent depth.[2]

This gap is consequential. Enterprise investigations — whether concerning insider threats, ransomware incidents, or regulatory compliance violations — increasingly encounter mixed file system environments in which storage pools, virtual disk images, and local volumes co-exist across NTFS and ReFS partitions. The present study responds to this deficit with a large-scale empirical assessment of comparative artifact recovery under controlled, reproducible conditions.

Key Finding: ReFS 3.7 introduces 14 previously undocumented artifact generation pathways that, if unrecognised by examiners, may result in material evidence being overlooked or misattributed in enterprise forensic investigations. A revised forensic workflow addressing these pathways is presented in Section 4.3.

2. Methodology

2.1 Experimental Design and Image Corpus

A total of 847 forensic images were generated across 14 experimental conditions using Autopsy 4.21, The Sleuth Kit 4.12, FTK Imager 4.7.1.2, and a bespoke ReFS Parser developed by the research team (ReFS-Parse v1.4, available under MIT licence at the project repository). Images were acquired from 12 identically configured physical workstations running Windows 11 24H2 (Build 26100.2314) across a 16-week experimental period from June to September 2024. Each workstation hosted dual-partition configurations: a 512 GB NVMe NTFS system volume and a 1 TB HDD ReFS data volume, replicating a common enterprise architecture observed in 34% of Fortune 500 IT environment surveys conducted in 2023.

All acquisitions were performed using hardware write-blockers (Tableau TX1) with MD5 and SHA-256 hash verification at acquisition and post-analysis stages. Experimental conditions were stratified across six anti-forensic pressure categories detailed in Table 1.

2.2 The Forensic Resilience Index (FRI)

The FRI is a composite metric computed as a weighted sum of four sub-scores: MFT completeness (w=0.35), $LogFile/journal continuity (w=0.25), timestamp accuracy against ground-truth event logs (w=0.25), and metadata structural integrity (w=0.15). Sub-scores are normalised to [0,1] using ground-truth comparisons against pre-image database records established prior to anti-forensic intervention. Inter-rater reliability was assessed across three independent examiner teams (Krippendorff's α = 0.887, indicating strong agreement).

3. Results

3.1 Overall FRI Distribution

NTFS volumes exhibited a mean Forensic Resilience Index of 0.647 (σ = 0.091, 95% CI [0.633, 0.661]) across all experimental conditions, compared to a mean FRI of 0.581 (σ = 0.114, 95% CI [0.563, 0.599]) for ReFS volumes. This difference of 0.066 was statistically significant (two-sample t-test: t(694) = 8.42, p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 0.64, indicating a medium-to-large practical effect). However, this aggregate figure masks important within-category variation detailed in Table 2.

Notably, under journal disruption conditions (Category 3), ReFS volumes demonstrated a statistically significant advantage over NTFS (Δ = −0.042, p = 0.003). This counterintuitive result is attributable to the ReFS integrity stream mechanism, which maintains independent block-level checksums that survive conventional fsutil USN journal manipulation. NTFS, by contrast, relies more heavily on the $LogFile structure, which is more susceptible to targeted disruption.

3.2 Novel ReFS Artifact Pathways

Analysis of the ReFS image corpus identified 14 previously unreported artifact generation pathways attributable to the B+ tree object table structure, block-clone semantics, and the integrity stream subsystem of ReFS 3.7. These include residual allocation table entries persisting after block-clone operations, orphaned object identifiers in the Object ID table following volume integrity scans, and time-ordered sequence markers in the allocator journal that survive conventional anti-forensic deletion procedures. Full technical characterisation of each pathway is provided in Supplementary Material S2.

4. Discussion

4.1 Practitioner Implications

Our findings challenge the prevailing assumption — documented in widely-used forensic training curricula including SANS FOR500 and Cellebrite Certified Operator materials — that newer file systems necessarily complicate forensic analysis. While ReFS does introduce significant challenges in conventional MFT-centric investigation workflows, its structural properties generate compensatory artifact opportunities that, once understood, may meaningfully enhance evidentiary yield in specific scenarios.

The statistically significant advantage of NTFS under VSS manipulation conditions (Δ = +0.112, p < 0.001) is particularly relevant for ransomware investigations, where shadow copy destruction is a near-universal feature of modern encryptors. Practitioners investigating Windows 11 environments should be aware that ReFS volumes — when used as primary storage targets for Hyper-V checkpoints — may offer degraded VSS-analogous recovery relative to NTFS predecessors.

4.2 Limitations

This study is subject to several limitations. First, all experiments were conducted in controlled laboratory environments; field conditions involve heterogeneous hardware configurations, incomplete acquisition scenarios, and legally constrained access procedures that may alter observed FRI values. Second, the ReFS-Parse tool, while validated against Microsoft's published specification, has not yet undergone full peer-independent validation against commercial tool outputs for all 14 novel artifact pathways. Third, the study period (June–September 2024) preceded the December 2024 Windows 11 feature update (Build 26100.2605), which introduced minor changes to ReFS cluster allocation behaviour not yet assessed.

4.3 Revised Forensic Workflow

Based on our empirical findings, we propose a revised forensic workflow for hybrid NTFS/ReFS enterprise environments consisting of seven stages: (1) pre-acquisition environment profiling, (2) targeted hardware write-blocker calibration for NVMe vs. HDD media, (3) dual-hash corpus acquisition, (4) parallel MFT and ReFS object table parsing, (5) cross-system timeline normalisation, (6) novel artifact pathway screening using ReFS-Parse, and (7) court-ready documentation with FRI confidence intervals. Detailed implementation guidance is provided in Supplementary Material S3.

5. Conclusion

This study provides the largest controlled empirical comparison of NTFS and ReFS forensic artifact recovery to date. The Forensic Resilience Index offers a standardised, reproducible metric that may serve as a foundation for future tool validation frameworks and expert witness qualification criteria. The 14 novel ReFS artifact pathways identified herein represent a material contribution to practitioner knowledge, with direct implications for enterprise investigations conducted under Windows 11 24H2 and Windows Server 2022. Future work will extend the FRI framework to Linux ext4 and APFS environments in cross-platform investigation scenarios.

Peer Review Summary

REVIEWER 1 (Anonymous) — ACCEPTED WITH MINOR REVISIONS

This is a well-executed empirical study addressing a genuine gap in the forensic literature. The FRI metric is clearly motivated and operationalised. The sample size (N=847) is commendably large. The authors should clarify whether the ReFS-Parse tool accounts for the deduplication B+ tree changes introduced in Windows Server 2022 22H2. Minor typographical issues in Table 2 footnotes.

REVIEWER 2 (Anonymous) — ACCEPTED WITH MINOR REVISIONS

Comprehensive and methodologically sound. The identification of 14 novel artifact pathways is the paper's strongest contribution. I recommend the authors include a worked case study demonstrating the revised workflow on a representative ransomware scenario to improve practitioner accessibility. The statistical analysis is appropriate and the FRI weighting rationale is adequately defended.

EDITOR RESPONSE

Both reviewers and the handling editor are satisfied with the authors' revisions. The paper is accepted for publication in Vol. 14(3) of JDFM. Congratulations to the authoring team on a valuable contribution to the field.
